## Reproduced failure modes and bounded mitigations

### 1. Other libraries expose `.from()`

An unscoped syntactic scanner cannot tell `knex.from('phantom')` from a
Supabase client with an unusual local name. The row-cap scanner can emit a
false `UNBOUNDED`; the column scanner can report `MISSING_OBJECT`.

**Mitigation:** optional repeatable `--client-root <identifier>` on both
scanners. It matches the syntactic root of the receiver, including
`supabase.schema('public').from(...)`. Tested with a Knex-style decoy and
Supabase-style roots. Without the flag, broad discovery stays unchanged.

**Residual risk:** this is a name filter, not import/type provenance.
Aliasing can cause either false positives or false negatives.

### 2. A query is consumed before its limit

An `await q` or `q.then(...)` may execute a Supabase thenable before a later
`q.limit(10)`; previously the scanner saw `LIMIT` and could miss the
earlier unbounded request.

**Mitigation:** identify such source-order observations and mark a previously
safe-looking `LIMIT`, `SINGLE` or `HEAD` as `EARLY_EXECUTION`. Keep
`UNBOUNDED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `UNKNOWN`, `RANGE_SINGLE`, and
`RANGE` classifications stable when they already convey risk.
Tested both `await q` and `q.then` before a limit and `await q` after it.

**Residual risk:** this is file-local syntactic ordering, not interprocedural
dataflow or a runtime execution proof. `Promise.all([q])`, helper-mediated
execution, closures, and control-flow paths need additional analysis.

## Unresolved questions

- Both arms of an exhaustive `if/else` may bound a query, but the scanner
  still reports `CONDITIONAL` conservatively.
- Query builders passed between functions are not resolved across call sites.
- The full hunting skill needs evaluation on externally chosen incidents and
  real-world repositories, with human-reviewed ground truth.
- A clean static scan does not prove a bug-free production system.
